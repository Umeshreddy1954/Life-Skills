# Tiny Habits


### Question 1

The most interesting idea for me was starting with a very small habit instead of trying to make a big change at once. For example, instead of flossing all your teeth, start by flossing just one tooth every day. Small actions can slowly become regular habits.

### Question 2

B = MAP means **Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt**. All three should come together for a habit to happen.

- **M – Motivation:** Having the desire to do something.
- **A – Ability:** Making the habit easy to do.
- **P – Prompt:** Having a reminder or trigger to do the habit.

I can make a habit easier by starting with a small action, connecting it to an existing routine and setting a reminder.

### Question 3

Celebrating after completing a habit makes us feel good and gives us a sense of achievement. This positive feeling encourages our brain to repeat the action. Even a small celebration can help us build a habit.

### Question 4

The most interesting idea for me was the British Cycling team's approach of making small 1% improvements in everything they did. Instead of looking for one big change, they focused on improving small things consistently. These small improvements added up and helped them achieve great results.

### Question 5

The book explains that we should focus on the person we want to become, not just the results we want to achieve. For example, instead of just setting a goal to solve coding problems, I should start seeing myself as someone who codes and learns every day.

### Question 6

To make a habit easier:
- Make it obvious by keeping things needed for the habit ready.
- Make it attractive by connecting it with something you enjoy.
- Reduce the effort required to start.
- Follow the two-minute rule and start with a very small action.

### Question 7

To make a bad habit harder:
- Hide or remove the things that trigger the habit.
- Make the habit less attractive.
- Add more steps or effort before doing it.
- Make the results of the habit less satisfying.

### Question 8

One habit I want to develop is solving coding problems regularly.

- Keep my laptop and study materials ready.
- Set a fixed time every day for coding practice.
- Start with at least one problem instead of setting a big target.
- Track my progress and celebrate completing my daily practice.

### Question 9

One habit I want to reduce is spending too much time scrolling on my phone.

- Keep my phone away while studying.
- Turn off unnecessary notifications.
- Remove distracting apps from my home screen.
- Set a time limit for social media.
- Track the time I spend on my phone and avoid using it without a purpose.
